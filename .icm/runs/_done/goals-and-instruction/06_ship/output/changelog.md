# Two or three goals, and the instruction that stands

_3 September 2026_

A patient's file said what you had recommended, but not what the two of you were actually working
on this month — nor the one sentence you keep repeating to them. Both lived between consultations,
in your head or in the last note you happened to write.

The console now holds both.

What you will notice:

- Two or three priority goals per patient, kept in your order and numbered on the page. A fourth is
  refused rather than accepted quietly: the point of a priority is that there are few of them.
- An optional starting point on each goal — where the person was the day you set it — so progress
  is read against something rather than remembered.
- A dated check-in whenever you want one: whether it moved, the simple measure of the day
  (« 6/10 », « 1 réveil »), and a word about why. The most recent one shows beside the goal; the
  rest of the trail is one click away.
- Goals are archived, not deleted. An archived one keeps its check-ins, sits in a quieter section
  of the same card, and can be reactivated when it comes back around.
- One standing instruction per patient, in your own words. Replacing it files the previous one with
  the date it was replaced, so what you asked and when is still readable months later. Clearing it
  leaves the trail intact.

None of this appears on the link you share with the patient. The card is yours, on the patient's
page, between their link and their recommendations.
