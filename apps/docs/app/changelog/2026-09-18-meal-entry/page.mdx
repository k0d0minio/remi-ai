# Your patients write their own meals now

_18 September 2026_

« Je vais manger » and « J'ai mangé » were the two sentences the patient link was built toward.
They work. A patient opens their link, taps Repas, writes what they are about to eat — or what
they just ate — and it lands in the journal you already read, attributed to them.

What that changes for you:

- **Meals arrive without you typing them.** A meal a patient writes appears in their journal
  marked « écrit depuis le lien », beside the ones you transcribed. The count of meals waiting for
  a reply already on the patient page now fills up on its own.
- **They can tell you before the meal, not only after.** A meal written ahead reads « Prévu »
  until they say they have eaten it, at which point it becomes the same entry marked « Mangé » —
  one meal, one line in the journal, however many times they touched it. That is the difference
  that makes an answer worth writing: a suggestion about tonight's dinner is still useful at six
  o'clock.
- **Every meal has a place for your answer.** Where you have written feedback, the patient reads
  it under their meal. Where you have not, they read that one is coming — so a meal they wrote
  never looks like a meal nobody saw.
- **Your reply has a shape.** The feedback box now suggests one: what is good, what is missing,
  one improvement, and why it matters for them. Overwrite it with whatever the meal needs — it is
  a prompt, not a form. It is there so that when REMI starts drafting these, its answers and
  yours read the same to the person receiving them.

Their side stays deliberately small. Text only, no photos. An optional moment of the day —
petit-déjeuner, déjeuner, dîner, collation — which they can leave off, because a meal with no
moment is still a meal. And the same ceilings the link has always had.

REMI does not answer a meal yet. That is the next piece, and it drops into the space you can
already see under every entry.
