# The same protocol, without typing it a tenth time

_18 September 2026_

Ten patients on « augmenter les protéines, favoriser les oméga-3, limiter les sucres raffinés » was
the same three lines typed ten times. Encoding one section is already one save rather than thirty —
but every line in it still started from nothing, on every patient. That is what changes here.

Open any of the three lists with **Modifier** and there are two new ways to fill it.

**Reprendre de …** picks one of your other patients and shows what they currently have of that
kind, with a checkbox on each. Tick what applies and the lines land in the list you are editing —
unsaved, exactly as if you had typed them, yours to adapt or drop before you press
**Enregistrer la section**. Cancel instead and neither patient has changed.

What comes across is the facts and only the facts. A supplement brings its dose and its moment,
because those are true of the supplement whoever takes it. What it does not bring is the sentence
you wrote for the other person — the « raison », the « pourquoi », the detail under a
recommendation — because that was written to them, and a justification saved unread onto someone
else is worse than an empty field. Those arrive blank for you to fill in, or leave.

**Enregistrer comme modèle** keeps a set you use often. It opens the lines you have on screen with
the personal sentences already cleared, so you can write the version that suits everyone, give the
set a name — « Base anti-inflammatoire », « Compléments fatigue » — and save it. **Insérer un
modèle** drops it into any other patient's list afterwards, the same unsaved way. A model can be
renamed or deleted, re-saving under a name you already use replaces that set once you confirm, and
a model is yours alone until you choose to share it with the rest of the console.

**Recettes** joins the first half: **Reprendre de …** there ticks the same recipes in the assign
list, with the note blank and today's date, because why you gave a dish to someone is written for
that person or not at all.

Taking lines from another patient is recorded in the journal, naming who they came from and how
many — at the moment you take them, not when you save, so it is there even if you change your mind
and close the section.
