# What is actually in a food

_17 September 2026_

REMI now holds the French food-composition table — the one ANSES publishes and dietitians work
from. Three and a half thousand foods, seventy-four constituents each, from protein and fibre to
the individual omega-3 fatty acids.

Two things follow from that.

- **You can look a food up.** « Aliments » in the console searches by name — accents optional, so
  _magnesium_ finds _Magnésium_ — and shows a food's full composition per 100 g, with its group and
  the confidence ANSES gives each figure.
- **The numbers stay the publisher's.** Where the table says _traces_, or _< 0,01_, or was never
  measured at all, the page says exactly that instead of rounding it to a tidy zero. A figure you
  are going to repeat to someone should be the one ANSES measured, or visibly not a figure.

This is the groundwork for choosing foods automatically. When a recommendation says « augmenter les
protéines » or « favoriser les oméga-3 », REMI can now read that as a question about the table —
which foods, ranked, minus the ones that do not suit this person — rather than something it has to
recall. Nothing generates recipes from it yet; that comes next.

Source: Table Ciqual 2025, ANSES — Observatoire des Aliments, under the Licence Ouverte 2.0.
