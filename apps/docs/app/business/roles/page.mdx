# Roles

Who the product serves, what each of them sees, and what each can change. Scope reads this page to
decide who a change is for; Design reads it for the lens a prototype is drawn through.

Three roles, and only two of them are a session in the product. The session carries which one,
because it decides the navigation, the landing route and what the query layer will answer. The
operator is not a product session at all — it lives in a separate deployment, with its own URL and
its own access control.

## Patient

The individual being accompanied. They are the first audience of V2 and, in this phase, the only
one with a surface of their own: the patient experience is built and validated before the
practitioner space is built at all.

- **What they are trying to do** — apply what their practitioner told them, in a week that already
  has a job, a kitchen and a Tuesday at 7pm in it. Not to manage a condition, and not to use an app.
- **How they arrive** — through their practitioner, who recommends REMI and carries the clinical
  relationship. Acquisition is the practitioner's, not a marketing funnel's. The terrain cohort
  Morgane accompanies uses REMI free — their feedback is the payment; patients joining from the
  December open day onward subscribe. No price is decided.
- **What they can see** — their own accompaniment and nothing else: their profile, the
  recommendations they were given, the daily hub, their meals, their micro-actions and their
  supplement journal. No other patient's data, nothing about the practice, and no clinical record —
  REMI is not the medical record.
- **What their link opens on** — their **today**, not a summary. « Aujourd'hui / cette semaine »
  leads, and always renders: first the challenge their practitioner is running with them — one
  habit at a time, with its start date — or a line saying none is running; then the goals they are
  working on; then « La consigne de la semaine », the week's consigne their practitioner wrote _to
  them_ (a separate field from the standing consigne she steers by, which is addressed to REMI and
  never shown here — the page shows nothing rather than falling back to it). Then the way in to the meal
  loop, the main recommendations — the first of each category, the same set the console calls
  « principales », so reordering there reorders here — the recipes they currently hold, the
  newest documents their practitioner put on the page, the essentials to keep in, the general
  feedback box, and the living summary last. Each of those blocks is a preview that links
  into its own segment, and a block whose segment is empty does not render at all.
- **Their documents** — the PDFs, images and links their practitioner puts on the page (her recipe
  PDFs, a personalised list, a meal feedback), newest first under « Documents », each with the day
  it was added; those classed as a recipe are listed under « Recettes » too, and one attached to a
  goal or a recipe sits beside it. A file opens through a link that expires after five minutes and
  only their own link can open it. They read documents; they never upload one.
- **Their messages** — one general place to say how the week went, in their practitioner's words:
  « Comment se passe votre accompagnement cette semaine ? Vous pouvez également indiquer ici ce que
  vous avez pensé des recettes, des challenges ou des recommandations proposées. » The box sits on
  the home and on « Messages », which also shows the whole thread newest first — what they wrote,
  and each reply under the name of whoever answered — and the day they last wrote. No comments on
  individual recipes or recommendations, nothing sent to them by email or notification; a message
  is never edited or deleted, and the thread goes with the patient.
- **What they can change** — what they log, the messages they write, their own pace and preferences, and their answer to
  the running challenge: « Challenge acquis », then « Prêt(e) pour le prochain » once acquired —
  each undoable until their practitioner closes the challenge, which freezes what they said. Never the
  recommendations or the frame those sit in; outside the frame REMI does not improvise, it says so
  and points back to the practitioner.
- **How they are identified** — by the link itself. The unguessable token in `/p/[token]` is the
  whole credential: it now accepts writes as well as reads (decision #2, 10 September 2026), and
  there is still no account, no password and no session. Everything written through it is
  attributed to that link's patient, marked `written_by: patient` on the tables the practitioner
  also writes, and recorded in the audit trail with a `patient` actor. Two consequences the page
  states rather than implies: anyone holding the link can write as the patient, which the page says
  to them in their own language so they can choose who to forward it to; and the link is ceilinged
  — ten writes a minute, a hundred a day, text only, no uploads. A link reaches its own patient's
  record and no other: a write naming a row that belongs to someone else is refused in the same
  words as a link that never existed, so one patient can neither read nor write another's. Patient accounts are parked in
  `beyond-december`, and that is where the question of whether a URL is a good enough credential
  for health-data writes gets answered.
- **Which app they live in** — `web`.

## Practitioner

The professional whose recommendations REMI extends, and the prescriber: a patient reaches REMI
because a practitioner sent them. They stay the clinical reference throughout — REMI applies, it
never prescribes. The braindump also makes them the paying customer through a practitioner-side
subscription; that model is parked with the practitioner space, and the revenue path in front of the
December open day is the patient subscription instead.

- **What they are trying to do** — keep their recommendations alive between consultations without
  adding to their workload, and open the next session from what actually happened rather than from
  what anyone remembers.
- **What they can do today** — set up a patient and encode that patient's profile and
  recommendations in the patient's own space. That is deliberate: it is what lets the real product
  be tested on real patients before a practitioner space exists.
- **What they will be able to see** — the patients they support, and nobody else: adherence at a
  glance, one patient's detail, and who is struggling. Access follows the care relationship and ends
  when that relationship does.
- **What they will be able to change** — the recommendations and the rules for a patient they
  support, remotely, with the accompaniment regenerated from them. Tightening what may be suggested
  is theirs alone and needs nobody else's say-so.
- **Which app they live in** — `web`. The dashboard and the rest of the practitioner space are
  parked until the patient experience is validated, and are re-specified from terrain experience
  when they are picked up; see [V2 scope](/business/scope).

## Operator

Internal, and today that means the founders. The role exists to run the platform and to be able to
answer for what it did.

- **What they are trying to do** — know which accounts exist and in what state, see what a
  practitioner or a patient is stuck on, and be able to say what changed and who changed it.
- **What they can see** — accounts and their status, the patient roster and the profiles and
  recommendations behind it. A patient's page opens on a working view — status, goals, the
  standing instruction, the summary head, recent meals, the latest messages with how many await a
  reply, the main recommendations, and a note for the next consultation — with every other section behind navigation: a section index on desktop,
  a segmented control (Suivi · Journal · Dossier · Profil) on phone. Behind that navigation the
  anamnesis shows what has been filled in first, the profile reads as a summary before it offers
  its form, and anything archived is a closed fold with its count at the foot of its section.
  Plus the support inbox,
  feature flags and the audit log. A practitioner's roster shows as activity — is anything moving
  on this account — rather than as plan content.
- **What they can change** — access to an account (suspend, restore, reset) and platform flags, with
  a confirmation and an audit entry on anything destructive. No commercial terms: there are none to
  render, and an operator who could set a price from a table is one mis-click from a term nobody
  agreed to.
- **How a protocol gets encoded a second time** — a section's rows can be filled from another
  patient's, or from a named set the operator has saved and can reuse anywhere. Two rules bound it.
  A copy carries the **factual** fields and arrives with the **personal** ones blank: a supplement's
  dose and moment travel, the « raison » written to one person does not. And reading one patient's
  rows into another's record is recorded in the audit trail, naming the source patient, at the
  moment of the copy rather than at the save — nothing is written to either patient until the
  section's own save. A saved set belongs to the operator who saved it and is private until she
  shares it with the console.
- **Which app they live in** — `admin`, a separate deployment. Never a route group inside the
  product, where every operator capability would be one routing mistake away from a customer.

## What each role sees, per app

| App                 | Patient                                                              | Practitioner                                         | Operator                                               |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| `web` (:3000)       | Their profile, the daily hub, meals, micro-actions, supplements — read and write, on their link | Their patients — parked until the patient half lands | Nothing — no operator session exists in the product    |
| `admin` (:3002)     | Nothing — the app has no public route                                | Nothing                                              | The console: accounts, patients, support, flags, audit |
| `marketing` (:3001) | The public site, signed out, in English or French                    | The same site, plus the practitioner page            | The same as anyone                                     |
| `support` (:3004)   | The help centre, anonymously — it never knows who is asking          | The same                                             | The same                                               |
| `docs` (:3003)      | Not a product surface — this site is reference material for the team | The same                                             | The same                                               |
| `demo` (:3005)      | Not a product surface — the prototype sandbox, mock data only   | The same                                             | The same                                               |

Two of those cells matter more than they look. `web` is the only app where the role changes what is
rendered, and `admin` is the only app no role but the operator can reach at all — that boundary is
the reason it is a separate deployment rather than a route group.

## Why this page is worth keeping accurate

Decision rights are the most common silent assumption in a scope. Written here once, a scope
document does not have to re-derive them, and a spec that gives a patient something only a
practitioner may change contradicts a page rather than an opinion.

The pipeline's label vocabulary carries the `persona:` axis this page defines — `patient`,
`practitioner`, `operator`, the same three names used above — as the `personas` list in
`.icm/project.json`.
