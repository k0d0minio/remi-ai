# Roles

Who the product serves, what each of them sees, and what each can change. Scope reads this page to
decide who a change is for; Design reads it for the lens a prototype is drawn through.

Three roles, and only two of them are a session in the product. The product's own vocabulary is
`practitioner` and `person`; the session carries which one, because it decides the navigation, the
landing route and what the query layer will answer. The operator is not a product session at all —
it lives in a separate deployment, with its own URL and its own access control.

## Person

The individual being accompanied. "Person" and not "patient": REMI sits beside a clinical
relationship rather than inside one, and the accompaniment belongs to them, not to a case file.

- **What they are trying to do** — apply what their practitioner told them, in a week that already
  has a job, a kitchen and a Tuesday at 7pm in it. Not to manage a condition, and not to use an app.
- **What they can see** — their own accompaniment and nothing else: today, meals, steps, and the
  plan behind them. No other person's data, nothing about the practice, and no clinical record —
  REMI is not the medical record.
- **What they can change** — what they log, and their own pace and preferences. Never the
  recommendations or the frame those sit in; outside the frame REMI does not improvise, it says so
  and points back to the practitioner.
- **Which app they live in** — `web`, landing on Today.

## Practitioner

The professional whose recommendations REMI extends. They stay the reference throughout: REMI
applies, it never prescribes.

- **What they are trying to do** — keep their recommendations alive between consultations without
  adding to their workload, and open the next session from what actually happened rather than from
  what anyone remembers.
- **What they can see** — the people they support, and nobody else. Access follows the care
  relationship and ends when that relationship does. Their practice overview, their client list, one
  client's plan and progress, and the therapeutic frame.
- **What they can change** — the therapeutic frame (what may be suggested, what must be avoided,
  and for whom) and the plan for a person they support. Tightening the frame is theirs alone and
  needs nobody else's say-so.
- **Which app they live in** — `web`, landing on Clients.

## Operator

Internal, and today that means the founders. The role exists to run the pilot and to be able to
answer for it.

- **What they are trying to do** — know who applied and who is onboarded, see what a practitioner is
  stuck on, and be able to say what changed and who changed it.
- **What they can see** — practitioner accounts and their status, the pilot's terms and enrolment,
  the support inbox, feature flags and the audit log. A practitioner's roster shows as activity — is
  anything moving on this account — rather than as plan content.
- **What they can change** — access to an account (suspend, restore, reset) and platform flags, with
  a confirmation and an audit entry on anything destructive. Not the pilot terms: those are fixed by
  a signed document and rendered rather than edited, because an operator who can change the price
  from a table is one mis-click from a term nobody agreed to.
- **Which app they live in** — `admin`, a separate deployment. Never a route group inside the
  product, where every operator capability would be one routing mistake away from a customer.

## What each role sees, per app

| App                 | Person                                                               | Practitioner                                          | Operator                                                 |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| `web` (:3000)       | Today, meals, steps, their plan                                      | Practice, clients, a client's plan, therapeutic frame | Nothing — no operator session exists in the product      |
| `admin` (:3002)     | Nothing — the app has no public route                                | Nothing                                               | The console: practitioners, pilot, support, flags, audit |
| `marketing` (:3001) | The public site, signed out, in English or French                    | The same site, plus the pilot application             | The same as anyone                                       |
| `support` (:3004)   | The help centre, anonymously — it never knows who is asking          | The same                                              | The same                                                 |
| `docs` (:3003)      | Not a product surface — this site is reference material for the team | The same                                              | The same                                                 |
| `demo` (:3005)      | Not a product surface — the Design stage's sandbox, mock data only   | The same                                              | The same                                                 |

Two of those cells matter more than they look. `web` is the only app where the role changes what is
rendered, and `admin` is the only app no role but the operator can reach at all — that boundary is
the reason it is a separate deployment rather than a route group.

## Why this page is worth keeping accurate

Decision rights are the most common silent assumption in a scope. Written here once, a scope
document does not have to re-derive them, and a spec that gives a person something only a
practitioner may change contradicts a page rather than an opinion.

The pipeline's label vocabulary can now grow the `persona:` axis it deferred while this page was a
stub — `person`, `practitioner`, `operator`, the same three names used above.
