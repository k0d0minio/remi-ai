# Roles

Who the product serves, what each of them sees, and what each can change. Scope reads this page to
decide who a change is for; Design reads it for the lens a prototype is drawn through.

Three roles, and only two of them are a session in the product. The session carries which one,
because it decides the navigation, the landing route and what the query layer will answer. The
operator is not a product session at all — it lives in a separate deployment, with its own URL and
its own access control.

## Patient

The individual being accompanied. They are the first audience of V2 and, in this phase, the only
one with a surface of their own: the patient experience is built and validated before the
practitioner space is built at all.

- **What they are trying to do** — apply what their practitioner told them, in a week that already
  has a job, a kitchen and a Tuesday at 7pm in it. Not to manage a condition, and not to use an app.
- **How they arrive** — through their practitioner, who recommends REMI and carries the clinical
  relationship. Acquisition is the practitioner's, not a marketing funnel's. The terrain cohort
  Morgane accompanies uses REMI free — their feedback is the payment; patients joining from the
  December open day onward subscribe. No price is decided.
- **What they can see** — their own accompaniment and nothing else: their profile, the
  recommendations they were given, the daily hub, their meals, their micro-actions and their
  supplement journal. No other patient's data, nothing about the practice, and no clinical record —
  REMI is not the medical record.
- **What they can change** — what they log, and their own pace and preferences. Never the
  recommendations or the frame those sit in; outside the frame REMI does not improvise, it says so
  and points back to the practitioner.
- **Which app they live in** — `web`.

## Practitioner

The professional whose recommendations REMI extends, and the prescriber: a patient reaches REMI
because a practitioner sent them. They stay the clinical reference throughout — REMI applies, it
never prescribes. The braindump also makes them the paying customer through a practitioner-side
subscription; that model is parked with the practitioner space, and the revenue path in front of the
December open day is the patient subscription instead.

- **What they are trying to do** — keep their recommendations alive between consultations without
  adding to their workload, and open the next session from what actually happened rather than from
  what anyone remembers.
- **What they can do today** — set up a patient and encode that patient's profile and
  recommendations in the patient's own space. That is deliberate: it is what lets the real product
  be tested on real patients before a practitioner space exists.
- **What they will be able to see** — the patients they support, and nobody else: adherence at a
  glance, one patient's detail, and who is struggling. Access follows the care relationship and ends
  when that relationship does.
- **What they will be able to change** — the recommendations and the rules for a patient they
  support, remotely, with the accompaniment regenerated from them. Tightening what may be suggested
  is theirs alone and needs nobody else's say-so.
- **Which app they live in** — `web`. The dashboard and the rest of the practitioner space are
  parked until the patient experience is validated, and are re-specified from terrain experience
  when they are picked up; see [V2 scope](/business/scope).

## Operator

Internal, and today that means the founders. The role exists to run the platform and to be able to
answer for what it did.

- **What they are trying to do** — know which accounts exist and in what state, see what a
  practitioner or a patient is stuck on, and be able to say what changed and who changed it.
- **What they can see** — accounts and their status, the patient roster and the profiles and
  recommendations behind it, the support inbox, feature flags and the audit log. A practitioner's
  roster shows as activity — is anything moving on this account — rather than as plan content.
- **What they can change** — access to an account (suspend, restore, reset) and platform flags, with
  a confirmation and an audit entry on anything destructive. No commercial terms: there are none to
  render, and an operator who could set a price from a table is one mis-click from a term nobody
  agreed to.
- **Which app they live in** — `admin`, a separate deployment. Never a route group inside the
  product, where every operator capability would be one routing mistake away from a customer.

## What each role sees, per app

| App                 | Patient                                                              | Practitioner                                         | Operator                                               |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| `web` (:3000)       | Their profile, the daily hub, meals, micro-actions, supplements      | Their patients — parked until the patient half lands | Nothing — no operator session exists in the product    |
| `admin` (:3002)     | Nothing — the app has no public route                                | Nothing                                              | The console: accounts, patients, support, flags, audit |
| `marketing` (:3001) | The public site, signed out, in English or French                    | The same site, plus the practitioner page            | The same as anyone                                     |
| `support` (:3004)   | The help centre, anonymously — it never knows who is asking          | The same                                             | The same                                               |
| `docs` (:3003)      | Not a product surface — this site is reference material for the team | The same                                             | The same                                               |
| `demo` (:3005)      | Not a product surface — the Design stage's sandbox, mock data only   | The same                                             | The same                                               |

Two of those cells matter more than they look. `web` is the only app where the role changes what is
rendered, and `admin` is the only app no role but the operator can reach at all — that boundary is
the reason it is a separate deployment rather than a route group.

## Why this page is worth keeping accurate

Decision rights are the most common silent assumption in a scope. Written here once, a scope
document does not have to re-derive them, and a spec that gives a patient something only a
practitioner may change contradicts a page rather than an opinion.

The pipeline's label vocabulary can now grow the `persona:` axis it deferred while this page was a
stub — `patient`, `practitioner`, `operator`, the same three names used above.
