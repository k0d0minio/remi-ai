Part one

What REMI actually is, in Morgane's own words

REMI is not a nutrition app. It is the "last kilometre" between a health practitioner and a patient's everyday life: the practitioner recommends, and REMI turns those recommendations into small, concrete, daily actions the patient can actually do. The whole product exists to close one gap — the gap between "I know what I should do" and "I actually do it."

Three things define the product now, and all three come from lessons learned the hard way with version 1:

  • The practitioner is the centre of everything. REMI started as an app sold directly to patients. It pivoted: practitioners (nutritionists, dietitians, functional-medicine doctors) are now the customers, the prescribers, and the acquisition channel. They pay a monthly subscription; their patients arrive through them — via a QR code or an invitation — at almost zero acquisition cost. Patients get a free version, with a paid premium tier later.
  • No more rigid meal plans. V1 imposed plans and asked users to fill a 7-day food diary before getting anything back. People quit. V2 flips the model: the user says what they're about to eat, and REMI instantly suggests a realistic improvement — adapted to their practitioner's recommendations, their tastes, budget, time, and family life — and explains why. Morgane's rule for V2: deliver value in under 60 seconds.
  • Small steps, not transformations. The unit of progress is the "micro-action": something doable in minutes ("tomorrow morning, swap your usual bread for buckwheat bread"), with the reasoning attached. Adherence over ambition.

What V2 must contain

The braindump is precise about this. On the patient side: an ultra-simple onboarding (the 7-day diary is explicitly killed), the central "Améliore mon assiette" feature ("improve my plate"), a daily hub, a "je mange autre chose" mode for when real life doesn't match the plan, a smart supplement journal, and micro-actions with their "why". On the practitioner side — which Morgane calls the most strategic build of all: a dashboard showing each patient's adherence at a glance, a detailed per-patient view, the ability to adjust recommendations remotely, quick encouragements, and group messages. And one technical centrepiece: a recommendation parser that reads a practitioner's PDF or consultation notes and turns them automatically into structured rules REMI can act on.

Part two

Where what we built diverges from her vision

Being straight about this matters more than being comfortable: everything built so far was built on assumptions about the product, and the braindump has now replaced those assumptions. Some of the work transfers well. Some of it doesn't.

What transfers

  • The engineering foundation is sound and reusable. Clean structure, good conventions, working email delivery, protected code review. None of that is wasted regardless of direction.
  • Supabase, which Morgane names as V2's base, fits. Her braindump says V2 runs on "Supabase, simplified AI workflows, targeted AI calls, cost control". The codebase was deliberately built so the database and login system could be plugged in later without rework — Supabase can be that plug. This also quietly resolves a debate that had been going back and forth in our docs (Neon vs Supabase): her document settles it — Supabase.
  • The general shape — a patient app plus a practitioner space plus an admin view — matches what she describes.

What doesn't

  • The old backlog was pointed at the wrong product. Its feature list was reconstructed from the deleted V1 code: a psychological scoring questionnaire, a genetics interpretation engine, weekly plan generation. None of those appear in Morgane's V2 priorities. Her V2 is "improve my plate", the daily hub, micro-actions, the practitioner dashboard, and the parser. (Genetics appears only as a far-future idea, "REMI Genetics".) Building the old list would have spent the ~€10k budget on the wrong things.
  • Six separate websites is heavier than a ~€10k, validate-fast project needs. The current setup runs six deployed apps (product, marketing site, admin, docs, support centre, demo). Morgane's priority №1 is radical simplicity and fast iteration. A leaner surface — product, practitioner space, one marketing page — deserves discussion. Not a decision for this report; a conversation for the call.
  • The "signed pilot" never existed. Demo data in the admin console (15 practitioners, €24.50/month, billing from 1 September) was mistaken by an earlier audit for a real contract and turned into a deadline. The braindump confirms the truth: the 15 practitioners are a beta recruitment target. There is no billing deadline and no revenue yet. All work items built on that fiction have been removed.

Q2 The arguments, structured so you can defend them in front of the jury

« …une explication claire et structurée des éléments qui permettent à REMI d'y répondre, afin que je puisse bien comprendre les arguments à mettre en avant et être capable de les défendre correctement devant le jury. »

Five arguments, in the order to make them. Each one is sourced from your own material, so you can defend every sentence.

  1. The problem is proven, and it's behavioural, not informational. "Patients don't lack information — they fail to apply it. We tested our first version with real patients from a functional-medicine clinic and watched exactly where they drop off: too much effort before any value, too rigid, too much mental load. That field learning is our unfair advantage — we know why health apps lose people."
  2. We occupy an empty space: the last kilometre. "Practice-management software manages the cabinet. Nutrition apps count calories. Nothing manages what actually happens between two consultations — whether the patient applies anything. REMI is built exclusively for that gap."
  3. The business model makes the practitioner our distribution. "We don't buy patients with ads. Practitioners subscribe (€39–199/month), and each brings their patient base through a QR code. Acquisition cost near zero, credibility built in, retention anchored in a human relationship. That's what makes the model replicable city by city, country by country."
  4. The technology in development is genuinely ours. "Two proprietary components: a parser that converts a practitioner's PDF or notes into structured, actionable rules — and a behavioural engine that turns those rules into daily micro-actions with the reasoning attached. Over time, the adherence data this generates — what people actually manage to change, and what makes them give up — becomes an asset nobody else in this market holds."
  5. Sovereignty is a design commitment we can demonstrate. "Health data stays in the EU. Our infrastructure and AI-provider choices are being made on that basis, and it's written into how the V2 is built — not retrofitted after growth."

And the honest answers to the two hard questions the jury will ask. "Isn't this just an app calling an AI model?" — "The model is a commodity; the parser, the behavioural method, and the practitioner–patient data loop are not. That's where the work and the value are." "What traction?" — "Field-tested MVP with clinic patients, validated practitioner demand from interviews, a founding-practitioner beta of ~15 launching with the V2, incubator support in place. Revenue starts with the V2 — that's precisely what this financing accelerates."

Part four

A new plan: phases for the next ticket set

The old backlog is gone. This is the proposed replacement, built from the braindump's own priorities — simplicity first, practitioner dashboard as the strategic centre, value in 60 seconds, ~€10k discipline. Once you've both reviewed it, each phase gets cut into concrete tickets.

Phase A · Decide & clear the ground — days

Align the project with the braindump

  • Adopt Supabase officially (your document names it; the code is ready to receive it) — one decision, closes an old internal debate.
  • Freeze the V2 scope to the braindump's feature list: improve-my-plate, daily hub, "je mange autre chose", supplement journal, micro-actions + why, practitioner dashboard, parser. Nothing else enters V2.
  • Decide how much of the current six-site setup survives — recommendation: product + practitioner space + one marketing page for now; park the rest.
  • Answer the Startup Boost gates (seat, incorporation date) and decide go / no-go on the application — before the dossier consumes September.
  • Settle the V1 estate: check the old Supabase for real patient data, close or claim the old vendor accounts.
Phase B · Foundation — 1–2 weeks

Real accounts, real data, safety nets

  • Connect Supabase: database + login (practitioner accounts, patient accounts, the invite/QR link between them).
  • Data model for the V2 loop: patient profile, practitioner, recommendation, rule, micro-action, feedback — matching the braindump's concepts, not V1's.
  • Data protection groundwork before the first real record: EU hosting, processing agreements, retention rules. Health data — non-negotiable.
  • Basic test coverage and crash reporting, so the beta doesn't debug itself through practitioners.
Phase C · The patient core loop — the heart of V2

Value in under 60 seconds

  • Ultra-simple onboarding — most-frequent-foods based, first micro-action generated immediately; no 7-day diary.
  • "Améliore mon assiette": user says what they'll eat → analysis against their practitioner's recommendations → one concrete improvement → the "why".
  • Daily hub + "je mange autre chose" mode (works with what's actually in the fridge).
  • Supplement journal with reminders; micro-action tracking that feeds the practitioner view.
  • AI cost discipline from day one: targeted calls, per-generation cost tracking, retry/error handling — your own stated priorities.
Phase D · The practitioner dashboard — the strategic build

What makes practitioners subscribe

  • Cohort view: all patients, adherence at a glance, who's struggling.
  • Per-patient detail: actions done, difficulties, food habits, feedback.
  • Adjust recommendations remotely; regenerate the patient's guidance.
  • Quick feedback (encouragements, emojis, short messages) and group messages to several patients.
  • QR / invite-link patient onboarding — the acquisition mechanism itself.
Phase E · The parser — the proprietary core

Practitioner documents → structured rules

  • Upload a PDF / consultation notes → extract recommendations → structured food rules → feed the patient loop automatically.
  • Start narrow (the formats your beta practitioners actually produce), widen later.
  • This is also the strongest "proprietary technology" exhibit for Startup Boost — building it early serves the pitch and the product at once.
Phase F · Money & the beta — validation

First revenue, real usage

  • Payments: practitioner subscriptions (Starter/Growth/Clinic tiers), patient premium later.
  • Recruit and onboard the ~15 founding practitioners; two-week feedback cycles, as your workflow describes.
  • Instrument the KPIs from your own list: daily/monthly actives, adherence rate, retention at J+7/30/90, practitioner usage.
  • Target: the braindump's own first milestones — 10–30 active practitioners, €2–5k monthly recurring revenue.
How Startup Boost fits this plan

If the application goes ahead, it runs in parallel with Phase A/B — it's writing and financial-projection work (Morgane + accountant + me for the technical annexes), not engineering. Phases C–E are precisely what the €100k would accelerate, which makes the funding ask easy to justify: "finish the execution engine and run the founding-practitioner beta at full speed."
