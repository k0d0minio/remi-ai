Correction — the pilot programme does not exist

Much of this repository is written as though fifteen practitioners had signed terms billing €24.50 each per month from 1 September. That programme is fiction. The numbers live in apps/admin/lib/fixtures.ts, a file whose own header describes it as "the console's data, standing in for reads that do not exist yet" — demo placeholders for a console with no backend. The entire admin pilot/ surface renders from them.

The pre-build audit read that fixture data as a real commercial contract and propagated it: finding F-07, owner decision D-4, the P0 ticket REMI-025 "Billing for 1 September", and three items in the requests register. REQ-20 even calls it "the signed pilot terms" in the same sentence that identifies it as fixture data.

What follows from that: there is no billing deadline, no revenue to invoice, and no contractual pressure on 1 September. REMI-025 should be closed or rewritten rather than worked. And — the part that actually matters — none of this may appear in the Startup Boost application. Traction is exactly what a jury probes hardest, and citing fifteen paying practitioners that do not exist would be a material misrepresentation to a public funding body. See Q2.

Q2 Could REMI meet the conditions for Startup Boost?

« As-tu également eu l'occasion de regarder le programme Start-up Boost ? Penses-tu que REMI pourrait remplir les conditions pour postuler ? »

Yes, plausibly — and the thematic fit is unusually good — but it turns on two hard eligibility gates neither of us has confirmed, and the deadline is 28 days away.

Startup Boost is Wallonie Entreprendre's early-stage instrument: a €100,000 convertible loan on standardised terms, roughly eight weeks from application to decision, minimum two startups selected per call. The current edition is explicitly an AI and cybersecurity call, and it closes 15 September 2026.

Neither is a judgement call — they are facts Morgane or Arnaud can answer in one line, and there is no point building a dossier before they do.

Where REMI argues strongly

  • Genuine proprietary IP, and it is the clinical layer. The Nutrition Mindset psychological scoring model (a 20-item instrument scored across seven dimensions into seven profiles), the nutrigenomic interpretation grid (ApoE × DIO2 × AMY1A), and the Guardian validation doctrine are real know-how that a competitor cannot copy from the interface. This is the deeptech argument, and it is honest.
  • A sector-specific solution to an identified need — exactly the phrasing the call uses — rather than a horizontal tool.
  • Technical replicability: a multi-tenant architecture, one deployment, six apps. The scalability criterion is straightforwardly answerable on the engineering side.
Do not cite the pilot

An earlier draft of this page listed "fifteen pilot practitioners with signed terms" as REMI's strongest traction claim. It is not real — see the correction at the top. Startup Boost is public money assessed by a jury of investors and operators who probe traction hardest of all, and a claim of fifteen paying customers would be checked. Presenting it would be a material misrepresentation, and the reputational cost of being caught at it in a small ecosystem outlasts any single application.

So the honest position on traction is: I have no verified commercial traction to point at. If real numbers exist — signed letters of intent, practitioners trialling the product, a waiting list — they are outside the repository and I need them from you. If they do not exist yet, the application argues on the models, the team and the market instead, which is a perfectly normal shape for an early-stage deeptech dossier.

Where a jury will push — and where we are exposed

This is the part worth being unsentimental about, because the call's exclusions are specific and REMI brushes against them.

  • The call explicitly excludes "projects primarily integrating third-party technologies without significant innovation", "generic chatbots relying on existing models", and technology-integration businesses. REMI's platform is assembled from standard tools; only the clinical models are proprietary. The application must therefore be built on the models and the doctrine, never on the stack.
  • The AI brain may not be ours. Plan generation and the Guardian validation run through a private Python API whose authorship I still do not know. If we did not write it, the deeptech claim is thin and a jury will find that out. This is the single most important thing to establish before drafting anything.
  • The sovereignty claim does not yet hold. The call's first criterion is control over key technologies, strategic data and critical infrastructure. The new codebase has decided an EU posture and leans toward pseudonymising personal data before it reaches any AI provider — but the v1 that actually ran in production sent generation through OpenRouter to a Google model and PDFs through a US parsing service. That gap between decided posture and deployed reality should be closed before it is claimed, not after.
Where Morgane's own list helps

Her mention of Mistral is interesting precisely here. A European model provider is a materially stronger sovereignty story than the American ones in the current architecture. If there is a reason she named it, it is worth hearing — it may be the missing half of the criterion-one argument.

Suggested reply: yes, it is worth applying and the thematic fit is strong — but confirm company age and Walloon registered seat first, and send the full call regulation rather than the landing page, since eligibility rules usually sit separately from the thematic criteria and are what actually disqualifies people. Flag honestly that the pitch needs a P&L, which needs your accountant — and settle between you what, if anything, we can truthfully claim as traction.

Handling note

Nothing in the credentials category should arrive as plain text in an email or a chat. Ask to be invited as a user wherever that is possible; for raw keys that cannot work that way, a one-time secret link or a shared password manager. And if any key has already been pasted into an email thread, it needs rotating rather than hiding — worth saying so explicitly, without making it sound like an accusation.

With the billing deadline gone, the month is much less crowded than it looked. One external deadline, one blocked pipeline, and a foundation that can resume at its own pace. The tension left is a real but manageable one: the grant application is business work, and it competes with the engineering that makes the product able to hold a patient record.

  1. Today — get CI green. The required Quality check is red on main, so branch protection is currently holding every merge. It looks like a stale @remi/ui build restored from the Turborepo cache rather than a source defect, so the fix is likely small. Nothing else can land until it clears.
  2. This week — clean the backlog of the fiction. Close REMI-025, retire D-4, and strike REQ-19/20/32 before the requests register goes to Morgane. Then settle D-2 on the database, which also answers authentication if it lands on Supabase. None of this needs code.

  4. Confirm Startup Boost eligibility before investing in it. Two one-line facts — company age and Walloon seat — decide whether the dossier is worth a month of attention at all.
  5. Then, and only then, restart the engineering. The board is correctly ordered: the test harness and error tracking (REMI-008, 009) come before the database adapter, because landing an adapter with no tests and no crash visibility is how the pilot finds the bugs for you. REMI-007 is P0 and cheap — do it first.
  6. Treat GDPR as a gate, not a task. This is special-category health data. The DPAs, retention policy and pseudonymisation posture need to exist before the first real record is stored, not in the sprint after.
The one thing to say out loud on the call

The repository has been describing a commercial reality that does not exist. Fifteen practitioners, signed terms, a billing date — all of it demo data that an audit mistook for fact and then built a P0 ticket and three information requests on top of. Nothing was done dishonestly; fixtures are exactly the right way to design a console with no backend. But the fiction propagated, and it very nearly went into a public funding application as a traction claim.

That is the thing to name early, because the correction is cheap now and expensive later. The real position is a strong, honestly-staged foundation with genuine clinical IP, no customers yet, and one grant deadline 28 days out.
